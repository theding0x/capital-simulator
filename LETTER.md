# The AI
Make no mistake, even though AI is marketed as a tool, it is a machine. The difference? A machine automates the use of many tools for a single craftsman, as opposed to many tools being used individually by the same craftsman (or multiple craftsmen) to create the same product. In the dev world, things like programming languages, databases, and the internet are the tools used to create a product like a web application and bring it to market. Like an industrial machine brings together and automates the tools to cut, shape, fill, seal, and label a can of tomato soup, the AI brings together and automates the tools to plan, scaffold, implement, test, persist, and visualize a system.

Machines don't just *do* this, though. They need to be properly configured and calibrated exactly to specifications so that tomato soup doesn't end up on the walls. AI is no different. It needs explicit instructions, concrete definitions and fixtures *in human language*, to complete its task successfully. So, with that in mind, let's get into the experiment.
## The Experiment
At this point, I am pretty familiar with the three volumes of Capital, but I am by *no means* an expert on Capital or Marxist theory. I am learning, and have been deep in research for the past year and a half. However, I would not be able to explain Capital to an AI well enough for an AI to generate anything but slop. I wouldn't, but Marx spent the course of decades mapping out what he saw to be the true, hidden nature of Capitalism. To do this, he used logic supported by concrete reported numbers to prove his tests passed. Most importantly, he wrote it all down in mountains of manuscripts explaining it, and thanks to some really great people at [marxists.org](https://marxists.org) , they are available for free.

So, I wanted to see what would happen if I let Marx drive Claude to build a simulation or demo of Marx's vision of Capitalism **in his own words**.
## The Process
For this, I went with a chapter-by-chapter approach from the beginning. Each session with the AI was limited in scope to one chapter. The AI's task was to read the chapter, extrapolate the primary logics, domains, and test cases into a separate spec file for review, then implement the spec with subagent driven development (planner/coder/reviewer stages). Once all chapters from a group of related chapters (called Parts in the text), an iterative reflection is run to make sure things aren't defined twice from chapter to chapter and check for cohesion in all the moving parts like database migrations.

The AI was explicitly told **NOT** to fill in any logical or functional gaps it may find. The goal is total fidelity to Marx's arguments and proposed laws of motion, for better or worse, with a data driven simulation of those laws in motion. All decisions made by me were superficial regarding how the UI was laid out and colored.
## The Results
My experiences with vibe coding in the past have typically led to frustration. I started this with the mentality that I would keep going until it broke. As the PR for the final chapter of Volume III merged and the iterative reflection stage completed over all three circuits with passes... I was stunned to see that it just... didn't. There was no friction. Chapter after chapter compiled perfectly and merged cleanly.
